patchwork runs the same way on one process and on many: without an MPI launcher, mpiarray hands
it a serial stand-in of a communicator, and every call below does its local work and skips the
messages. Under MPI, three ideas organise all of the communication:

1. **The hierarchy is the same on every rank; the data is not.** Every rank builds the same
   patches with the same calls in the same order, so each rank knows every patch, its level,
   its box and its ranks. A patch's *field* lives only on the patch's ranks, as an mpiarray
   array split over them.
2. **Schedules, built once and executed as one `Alltoallv`.** Every exchange between patches
   (halos, restriction, the transfer of a regrid) is a list of box copies between arrays on
   different groups of ranks. mpiarray's copy plan turns the list into one `Alltoallv` over the
   hierarchy's communicator, after a single `allgather` at the build. patchwork keeps the plans
   per field until the patches change.
3. **Two kinds of halo.** The halo at the *walls* of a patch is filled from the rest of the
   hierarchy by patchwork; the halo *between the blocks* of one patch is mpiarray's business,
   with point-to-point messages inside the patch's own communicator.

The rest of this page goes through the operations one by one. The [explorer](#try-it) in the
middle lets you change the number of ranks, the placement and the periodicity of a small
hierarchy and shows what the halo exchange then sends. A [table](#summary) at the end lists
every operation with its MPI calls, and a [checklist](#writing-code-that-runs-under-mpi) gives
the rules for code that uses patchwork under MPI.

## Ranks, patches and communicators

![The patches of a hierarchy coloured by the rank that holds each block, and the tree of communicators: the hierarchy's communicator above, one per group of ranks below](/patchwork/figures/mpi/ranks.svg)

`AMRHierarchy(..., comm=...)` takes the communicator of all ranks that take part (by default
`MPI.COMM_WORLD`, or the serial stand-in). Each patch then lives on a *group* of those ranks:

- `placement="all"` (the default) puts every patch on every rank;
- `placement="balanced"` puts a patch on the one rank that holds the fewest cells so far
  (ties go to the lower rank), and with `max_cells_per_rank=n` a patch of more than `n` cells is
  split over as many of the least loaded ranks as it needs;
- a function `placement(level, box)` returns the ranks, and `add_patch(..., ranks=...)` or
  `base_ranks` name them outright.

The placement is a pure function of the calls made so far, so every rank arrives at the same
answer without a message. For each group of ranks the hierarchy makes **one communicator**, the
first time the group is used, and keeps it in a cache that the patches share (and that the new
hierarchy of a [regrid](#regridding) inherits):

| group of ranks               | communicator                          | collective?                         |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| all ranks                    | the hierarchy's own `comm`            | no                                  |
| one rank                     | `MPI.COMM_SELF` on that rank          | no                                  |
| some ranks                   | `comm.Split(color, key=rank)`         | yes, over `comm`, once per group    |

On a rank outside the group, `patch.comm` is `None`, `patch.is_local` is `False` and
`patch.field(name)` raises: the patch exists there as a description only.
`hierarchy.local_patches` lists the patches this rank holds a part of.

`allocate_field(name, halo=h, split=0)` creates, on every patch this rank holds, an
`mpiarray` array over the patch's communicator: split along the first axis over the patch's
ranks (`split=0`, the default; a rank then needs at least one cell along that axis) or
**replicated** on all of them (`split=None`, every rank holding all of it). Both carry `h`
halo cells (or nodes) around the array. `gather(patch, name)` returns the whole array of one
patch on every rank, or on `root` only: it is collective over the hierarchy, as the first rank
of the patch broadcasts the array (`bcast`) or sends it to `root` (`send`/`recv`).

## Blocks and the two kinds of halo

![A 12 by 8 patch with halo 1 split over three ranks: the blocks, the halo at the patch walls filled by the copy plan, the halo between blocks filled by update_halos, and the storage of the middle rank](/patchwork/figures/mpi/blocks.svg)

Within a patch, mpiarray gives each rank a **block**: a near-even share of the first axis (the
first `nx mod size` blocks have one cell more), `layout.index_bounds` in the patch's indices.
`local` is the block, `local_with_halos` the block with its halo. The halo cells of a block
are of two kinds, and two different pieces of code fill them:

- at the **walls of the patch**, where the block's halo lies outside the patch: patchwork
  fills them from the other patches (see [below](#filling-the-halos)), through the copy plan;
- along the **seams between blocks**: mpiarray's `update_halos` copies the neighbour's boundary
  layer across, with one non-blocking send and receive per direction and per seam on the
  patch's communicator (`Isend`/`Irecv` of a subarray datatype, in place; corners travel with
  the faces because the layer spans the whole storage along the other axes).

The copy plan writes halo cells at the walls on the rank whose block touches that wall: a
rank's **receiving region** is its block, extended by the halo width where the block is at
the start or the end of the patch along an axis. So the halo along the unsplit axis belongs to
every block, and the halo along the split axis to the first and the last block only; the
corners at the seams come with the second kind of halo.

## The transport: mpiarray's copy plan

![The build of a copy plan: the source box of an array on two ranks and the target box of another, their pieces by pair of ranks; and the execution: send buffers by destination, one Alltoallv, receive buffers by source, unpack](/patchwork/figures/mpi/copy_plan.svg)

Every exchange between patches is a list of **copies**, each a
`(source, source_box, target, target_box)`: two mpiarray arrays (or `None` on a rank that
does not hold one) and a box of each, in its global indices, of the same extents. The target
box may reach into the target's halo at its walls. `mpiarray.CopyPlan(copies, comm)` is
built collectively over the hierarchy's communicator, with the same list on every rank:

1. For each copy, a rank works out the **block it sends from** (its block of the source; a
   replicated source sends from its first rank only) and the **region it receives into** (its
   receiving region of the target; every rank of a replicated target receives all of it).
2. One **`allgather`** tells every rank these blocks and regions of all the others. Their
   overlaps, shifted from the source box to the target box, are the pieces: which cells of
   which array go from which rank to which rank. Their sizes give the counts and offsets of
   the `Alltoallv`, fixed from now on.
3. An error (a box outside its array, boxes of different shapes, different numbers of copies
   on different ranks, different dtypes) is raised on every rank, after the `allgather`, so no
   rank is left waiting.

`plan.execute()` then does the same thing every time: it **packs** its pieces into one send
buffer, ordered by destination rank, makes one **`Alltoallv`**, and **unpacks** the receive
buffer, piece by piece, into `target.local_with_halos`, in the order of the copies (where the
target boxes of several copies overlap, the last copy wins). All sources are read before any
target is written, so a copy from an array to itself is safe. On one rank the receive buffer
is the send buffer and no MPI call is made. The arrays can be on the GPU: the buffers are the
backend's, and the exchange goes through staging buffers where MPI is not CUDA-aware.

patchwork builds these plans:

| schedule                                 | built by                       | plans                                  |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| `HaloExchange` (`exchange_halos`)        | first call for a field         | one                                    |
| `Restriction` (`restrict`, `reflux`)     | first call for a field         | one per fine level                     |
| `transfer` (`regrid`)                    | every regrid, for every field  | one per level                          |

The schedules of a field are kept in the hierarchy under their kind and name, and dropped when
a patch is added: the next call builds them again. The build has its own profiling region,
`patchwork.build_schedule`.

## Filling the halos

`exchange_halos(name)` fills the halo of every patch of a field. The schedule it builds finds,
for every halo cell (or node) of every patch, where the value comes from:

![A hierarchy with a periodic x axis: the halo cells of one patch coloured by their source: a patch of the same level, its periodic image, the interpolation of the level below, the other block of the patch, or outside the domain](/patchwork/figures/mpi/halo_sources.svg)

1. **A patch of the same level** that covers the halo cell, periodic images included (a
   patch that spans a whole periodic axis takes its halo from itself): a copy of the cell.
2. Otherwise, inside the domain and on a refined level, the **interpolation of the level
   below**: bilinear (linear in 1D) for the halo exchange, with the weights of coarse points
   on no patch dropped and the rest renormalised, and the two inside points extrapolated next
   to a wall.
3. **Outside the domain** the halo cell is left as it is, for the boundary conditions.
4. The halo cells along the **seams between the blocks** of a patch are not in the schedule:
   mpiarray fills them afterwards.

The interpolation needs coarse values on the ranks of the fine patch, and those ranks may hold
none of the coarse patch. For each fine patch, the schedule allocates a **buffer**: a
replicated array (`split=None`) on the fine patch's communicator, of the bounding box of the
coarse stencils of the uncovered halo cells. The copies that fill it, from every coarse patch
that overlaps the box (across periodic faces too), go into the same plan as the copies of the
first kind. Each rank of the fine patch receives the whole buffer and computes the weighted
sums for the halo cells it stores, with no further message:

![The coarse buffer of the interpolation: the halo cells of a fine patch, the two-by-two coarse stencils of two of them, one next to a wall and clipped, and the dashed buffer box that the plan fills](/patchwork/figures/mpi/interpolation.svg)

So `execute` is three steps, in this order: the plan's one `Alltoallv`, the local
interpolation from the buffers, then `update_halos` on every local patch's array. The
`level=` and `below=` arguments of `exchange_halos` (the halos of one level only, or the
level below taken from another field, for subcycling) are separate schedules, kept under their
own keys.

## Restriction

![Restriction of a fine patch on one rank onto a coarse patch split over two ranks: the fine values go into a replicated buffer on the coarse patch's ranks, each of which averages the two-by-two groups and writes the cells of its own block](/patchwork/figures/mpi/restriction.svg)

`restrict(name)` gives the coarse points under finer patches the fine values: the mean of the
fine cells of each coarse cell (weighted by their volumes in $(r, z)$), or the coinciding fine
node. It runs **finest level first**, with one plan per fine level, so that a level hands on
what it received from the level above. For every fine patch and every coarse patch under it,
the schedule allocates a replicated buffer on the *coarse* patch's communicator for the fine
region, and one copy fills it. After the plan's `Alltoallv`, every rank of the coarse patch
holds the fine values and averages them, but writes only the cells of its own block; the
halos are left for `exchange_halos`. `restrict(name, level=L)` runs the plan of level
$L + 1$ only.

## Deposit, migration and accumulation

A particle code keeps its markers on the ranks that hold their patches, deposits their charge
patch by patch, and then sums the copies of every node. Three methods, with three kinds of
communication:

![Three panels: markers coloured by the rank that owns them with arrows for the ones that migrate; a marker next to a seam depositing into the halo nodes of its block; the copies of a node at a coarse-fine edge and across a periodic seam sending their charge to the owner copy](/patchwork/figures/mpi/deposit.svg)

**Owners.** `owner(x, y, name)` gives the rank each marker belongs to: the rank that holds the
block of field `name` in which the lower corner of the marker's cell lies, in the finest
patch that holds it. The patch comes from the locator tables, built on every rank from the
hierarchy alone; the block comes from a table of every rank's `index_bounds` for the field,
gathered with **one `allgather`** the first time a field is used (kept as a schedule, like
the others). The same points give the same owners on every rank.

**Migration.** `migrate(x, y, *arrays, name=...)` sends each marker, with any per-marker
arrays, to its owner, with `mpiarray.migrate`: an `alltoall` of the row counts, then **one
`Alltoallv` per array**, as raw buffers without pickling. Markers outside the domain are
counted with an `allreduce` and raise on every rank (or are dropped with `outside="drop"`).
Call it again on the hierarchy a regrid returns, for the markers whose patch changed ranks.

**Deposit.** `deposit(x, y, charge, name)` is local work: each marker spreads its charge over
the corners of its cell with mpifield's compiled kernels, in this rank's block of the finest
patch that holds it, halo nodes included. Its one collective call is the check that every
marker is on its owner (an `allreduce` of a count), so that no deposit is silently lost.
`interpolate` is the transpose and has the same check; it needs the halos filled first.

**Accumulation.** After the deposit, a node's charge is spread over several copies: the halo
nodes of the blocks, and the copies of a node that several patches hold (the corners that
touch or nest, and the two copies of a node on a periodic seam). `accumulate_halos(name)`
brings it all to one copy, in two steps:

1. on every local patch, mpiarray's `accumulate_halos` adds the halo nodes of each block
   into the neighbouring blocks and zeroes them (`Isend`/`Irecv` on the patch's communicator;
   at the walls of the patch the halo is dropped);
2. the charge of every copy of a node goes to the **owner copy**: the one in the finest patch
   that holds the node (the last of that level in the order of the patches; on a periodic
   seam, the upper copy). The owners come from one table of the finest lattice of the whole
   domain, painted patch by patch from the coarsest, built on every rank without a message.
   The moving charges, with their patch numbers and node indices, travel in **one
   `mpiarray.migrate`** to the rank that holds the owner's block, and are added there.

The other copies end up zero, the total is kept, and `CompositeNodes.from_patches` then reads
the owner copies. On one rank the same paths are followed without MPI.

## Regridding

![Old and new level-1 patches of a regrid: the cells of the new patches that an old patch of the level covers are copied, the others interpolated from the new level below](/patchwork/figures/mpi/regrid.svg)

`regrid(tags)` builds a new hierarchy and moves every field to it:

1. **Tags.** Each rank tags from the data it holds; `combine_tags` combines them with one
   **`Allreduce` (max)** per level, so every rank clusters the same cells.
2. **The new hierarchy.** Every rank clusters the tags into the same boxes and adds the same
   patches, placed by the hierarchy's `placement` (the largest first), so the new hierarchy
   is again identical everywhere. It shares the cache of communicators, so a group of ranks
   that already had one makes no new `Split`.
3. **The transfer**, level by level from the coarsest: a point of a new patch takes the value
   of an old patch of the same level that has it, else the interpolation of the *new* level
   below (bilinear, or the conservative fill for a conservative field, which is first
   restricted on the old hierarchy). The copies from the old patches and those into the
   interpolation buffers make **one plan per level**, executed before the next level is
   built, since that level interpolates from this one.

Halos are not filled unless `fill_halos=True`; markers are moved with `migrate` on the new
hierarchy by the caller.

## Refluxing, integrals and the composite solves

**Refluxing.** `FluxRegister.advance` is local: each rank advances its blocks and records
the fluxes through the coarse-fine faces of its cells, times their weight. `reflux` sums the
records of all ranks with **one `allreduce`** of a vector with one value per coarse face
(the coarse and the fine side of a face can be on different ranks), corrects the coarse cells
each rank holds, and then restricts.

**Integrals.** `integral(name)` sums the leaf cells of this rank's blocks (a replicated field
counts once, on the first rank of its patch) and **`allreduce`s** the total.

**Composite solves.** `CompositeNodes` and `CompositeCells` are built from the description of
the hierarchy, the same on every rank, without a message. `from_patches(name)` gathers the
field of **every patch to every rank** (`gather`: one `bcast` per patch that is not on all
ranks), so that the right-hand side of a solve is complete everywhere. With the `"lu"` and
`"fft"` backends every rank then assembles and solves the whole system; `to_patches` writes the
solution back into the blocks locally. With the `"petsc"` backend, each node has an owner,
`node_ranks`: the lowest rank among those that hold the leaf cells around it. Every rank
assembles **only the rows of the nodes it owns**, in a numbering in which each rank's unknowns
are contiguous (an `allgather` of the row counts gives the offsets), into a PETSc `MPIAIJ`
matrix over the hierarchy's communicator, and PETSc's Krylov solve and multigrid
preconditioner do the rest; a `Scatter.toAll` brings the solution to every rank at the end.

## Summary

| operation                          | once                                                 | every call                                                          |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `AMRHierarchy`, `add_patch`        | `comm.Split` per new group of some ranks             | none (the same calls on every rank)                                 |
| `allocate_field`                   | none                                                 | none                                                                |
| `gather`                           | none                                                 | `bcast` from the first rank of the patch, or `send`/`recv` to root  |
| `exchange_halos`                   | `allgather` (the plan)                               | one `Alltoallv`; `Isend`/`Irecv` inside each split patch            |
| `restrict`                         | `allgather` per fine level                           | one `Alltoallv` per fine level                                      |
| `owner`                            | `allgather` (the blocks of the field)                | none                                                                |
| `migrate`                          | as `owner`                                           | `allreduce` (markers outside), `alltoall`, one `Alltoallv` per array |
| `deposit`, `interpolate`           | as `owner`                                           | `allreduce` (the check of the owners)                               |
| `accumulate_halos`                 | as `owner`                                           | `Isend`/`Irecv` inside each split patch; `alltoall` + `Alltoallv`s  |
| `regrid`                           |                                                      | `Allreduce` per tagged level; `allgather` + `Alltoallv` per level   |
| `FluxRegister.reflux`              | as `restrict`                                        | `allreduce` of the face records, then as `restrict`                 |
| `integral`                         | none                                                 | `allreduce`                                                         |
| `CompositeNodes.from_patches`      | none                                                 | `bcast` per patch                                                   |
| `NodalPoisson(backend="petsc")`    | `allgather` of the row counts; PETSc's setup         | PETSc's solve; `Scatter.toAll`                                      |

Every row is **collective**: all ranks of the hierarchy's communicator call it, whether they
hold the patches involved or not.

## Writing code that runs under MPI

- Build the hierarchy with the **same calls in the same order** on every rank: `add_patch`,
  `allocate_field`, `regrid` and the methods of the table above are collective. A rank that
  skips one deadlocks the others.
- Loop over `local_patches` for data and over `patches` for geometry; test `patch.is_local`
  before `patch.field(name)`. Use `gather` for the whole array of a patch (every rank calls
  it) and `local_node_coordinates` or `local_cell_centers` for the points of this rank's block.
- Fields that markers are deposited into need `split=0` (the default): a replicated field has
  no owner for a point. A replicated field is counted once by `integral`, `restrict` and the
  flux register (its first rank).
- Keep the hierarchy object: its schedules are built at the first call for each field and
  reused until a patch is added. `regrid` returns a new hierarchy and leaves the old one
  intact; move the markers with `migrate` afterwards.
- Compute on `local`, read halos from `local_with_halos` after `exchange_halos`; write only
  the block, the halos belong to the exchange.
- Launch with `mpirun -n 4 python script.py` after `pip install patchwork-amr[mpi]`, and
  profile the regions `patchwork.exchange_halos`, `patchwork.restrict`,
  `patchwork.build_schedule`, `patchwork.migrate`, `patchwork.deposit`,
  `patchwork.accumulate_halos`, `patchwork.regrid` and `patchwork.regrid_transfer` with
  scope-profiler (`--enable-tag patchwork`).

The figures of this page are drawn by `docs/mpi_figures.py` (`make docs-figures`); the cell
classifications in them are computed with patchwork's own geometry and checked against the
schedules it builds.
