patchwork is a block-structured adaptive mesh in one or two dimensions: nested rectangular
patches of cells, each finer by the refinement ratio $r$ (2 by default), each patch an mpiarray
array on its own ranks. On the *composite mesh*, the leaf cells that no finer patch covers, it
solves elliptic problems for a particle-in-cell or fluid code: a node-centred Poisson equation
for the potential and a cell-centred operator with variable coefficients, in the Cartesian
$(x, y)$ or the axisymmetric $(r, z)$ plane. Around them are the pieces a time-dependent code
needs: halo exchange, restriction, regridding, refluxing, and the deposit and gather of markers,
with an edge space whose current deposit conserves the charge exactly.

Each equation on these pages is declared on the code that implements it, as a method card, and
checked by the tests: ten closed forms against SymPy rules and four discretisations against
their order of convergence on manufactured solutions. The pages of this section list the cards
module by module; this one shows how they fit together.

## The pipeline

1. **[Hierarchy](/patchwork/numerics/mesh-hierarchy/).** Level $L$ has the spacing $h_L = h_0 / r^L$;
   patch boxes are aligned to the level below and covered by it. In $(r, z)$ a cell is a ring
   with its exact volume and face areas. Markers are deposited with cloud-in-cell weights in the
   finest patch that holds them, and the copies of a node are summed by the
   [deposit accumulation](/patchwork/numerics/mesh-accumulate/).
2. **[Halos and restriction](/patchwork/numerics/mesh-halos/).** A halo takes the values of the
   patches of its level, else the bilinear interpolation of the level below; restriction gives a
   coarse cell the volume-weighted mean of its fine cells. How the values travel between the
   ranks is the subject of the [MPI communication](/patchwork/guides/mpi-communication/) guide.
3. **[Regridding](/patchwork/numerics/mesh-regrid/).** Cells are tagged where a field changes by more
   than a threshold, covered with boxes by the Berger-Rigoutsos clustering, and the fields moved
   to the new hierarchy, conservatively where they must be.
4. **[Nodal Poisson](/patchwork/numerics/solvers-nodal-poisson/).** $-\nabla^2\phi = f$ on the nodes of the leaf
   cells: assembled cell by cell, with the hanging nodes of coarse-fine interfaces eliminated by
   their interpolation, and Shortley-Weller rows at embedded surfaces. With
   `mass="consistent"` the same assembly gives the Q1 finite elements instead of the
   five-point stencil ([mass matrices](/patchwork/numerics/spaces-mass/)):

<MethodCard name="patchwork.solvers.nodal_poisson.NodalPoisson" headingLevel={3} />

5. **[Cell operator](/patchwork/numerics/solvers-cell-operator/).** $-\nabla\cdot(a\nabla u) + c\,u = f$ on the
   leaf cells, assembled from an energy so that it stays symmetric (or, with conservative
   coarse-fine faces, conservative face by face), with the curl-curl of an azimuthal vector
   potential in $(r, z)$ and fixed or floating bodies.
6. **[Edge space](/patchwork/numerics/spaces-edges/).** The lowest-order (Whitney) edge interpolant on
   the leaf cells, whose lumped (consistent) mass matrix gives back the nodal operator's
   five-point stencil (its Q1 stiffness), and the current
   deposit along straight paths that conserves the charge exactly:

<MethodCard name="patchwork.spaces.edges.CompositeEdges.deposit_paths" headingLevel={3} />

7. **[Face space](/patchwork/numerics/spaces-faces/).** The lowest-order Raviart–Thomas
   faces (fluxes), with the divergence to the leaf cells and, in 3D, the curl from the edges:
   `C G = 0` and `D C = 0` exactly, also across patch faces.
8. **[Curl–curl](/patchwork/numerics/solvers-curl-curl/).** $\nabla\times(\nu\nabla\times\mathbf{A}) + \beta\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{J}$
   on the edges, lumped or consistent, solved by LU or by conjugate gradients with hypre's
   AMS.
9. **[Refluxing](/patchwork/numerics/mesh-reflux/).** For an explicit, conservative time step, the
   coarse cells next to finer levels take the fine fluxes instead of their own (Berger and
   Colella), with or without subcycling.

The assembled operators go to a **[solver backend](/patchwork/numerics/solvers-backends/)**: a direct LU
on every rank, fast transforms on a uniform mesh, or the
[distributed solve](/patchwork/numerics/solvers-distributed/) with PETSc's Krylov methods and algebraic
multigrid. The [legacy cell Poisson](/patchwork/numerics/solvers-poisson/) solve is kept for its users.

## Units and conventions

The operators carry no physical constants: for a charge density pass $\rho / \varepsilon_0$ as
the source, and $\mu_0$ goes into the coefficients of the cell operator. Fields are per unit
length out of the plane in $(x, y)$, totals over the ring in $(r, z)$, and per unit area on a
line. A node-centred deposit holds charges, not densities. Face conditions are named
`x_lower`, `x_upper`, `y_lower`, `y_upper` and `inner` (the edges of a domain that does not
fill its rectangle); an unlisted face is grounded.

## Not in the model

- No time integration or particle push: the fluxes of refluxing and the markers are the caller's.
- The cell operator has the LU only, on every rank, and is 1D and 2D only.
- No per-axis or per-level refinement ratios, no non-rectangular patches.
- The edge space is Cartesian only (1D, 2D, 3D); the nodal Poisson solve uses Cartesian control
  volumes also on an $(r, z)$ hierarchy.
- The Shortley-Weller rows are used only at regular nodes; elsewhere, and for the cell operator,
  an embedded surface is a two-point flux (about first order in the maximum norm).

## Verification

Besides the checks of the cards, the tests reproduce linear potentials exactly across patches,
compare the FFT and PETSc backends with SuperLU, solve a current loop and a conducting column in
a solenoid against their closed forms in $(r, z)$, conserve the integral to round-off through
refluxing, restriction and regridding, and conserve the charge of 500 random paths per patch
layout through the edge deposit.
